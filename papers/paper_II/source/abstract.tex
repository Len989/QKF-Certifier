\begin{abstract}
A finite equational presentation determines both an equality theory and the
resources required to recover that equality on a bounded set of terms. We
study the least horizon $\delta_E(d)$ at which congruence reasoning becomes
exact on closed terms of depth at most $d$, and the pairwise threshold
$\lambda_E(s,t)$. For finite ground presentations, subterm locality gives
$\delta_E(d)\le\max\{d,h(E)\}$, where $h(E)$ is the maximum axiom-side depth.
The possible low-depth profiles at exact axiom depth $h$ are precisely the
weakly increasing sequences $a_0\le\cdots\le a_{h-1}$ with $i\le a_i\le h$;
there are $C_{h+1}$ such profiles. An incrementally activated query-subterm
DAG computes the complete finite-query filtration. Chronological proof
DAGs and finite threshold countermodels provide complementary certificates
of the exact horizon. We apply this construction to the incomplete-action
interface of Paper I.

We distinguish this congruence-proof resource from the maximum depth in a
sequential rewrite derivation, denoted by $\lambda_E^{\mathrm{rw}}$ and
$\delta_E^{\mathrm{rw}}$. Explicit examples separate the two, even for
finite ground equations. For finite universal presentations, computability
of either visibility function is equivalent to decidability of the ground
word problem. Universal action-chain identities reproduce semigroup
\emph{rewrite} space exactly on typed chains. In the one-sorted language,
skeleton invariance yields the exact formula
$\delta_{\mathcal A(P)}^{\mathrm{rw}}(n)
=\max_{1\le m\le n}(n-m+s_P(m))$.
This separates the finite-ground certificate theory from the sequential
space results while retaining their common equational semantics.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Equational logic; congruence closure; equality
visibility; ground presentations; proof certificates; term rewriting;
semigroup derivational space.
