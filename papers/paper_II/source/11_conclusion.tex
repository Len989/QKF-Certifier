\section{Conclusion}

Equality visibility records the difference between the depth of a question and the depth required to establish its answer in a specified proof format. For congruence proofs over finite ground input, locality gives a finite exact horizon, all admissible low-depth profiles occur, and their spectrum is Catalan. A single activated input/query DAG computes first-equality levels. Positive proof DAGs and finite lower-threshold models together certify their exactness.

The common action example connects this filtration to Paper I: the named interface has five classes at horizon one and three at horizon two, while quotient-feedback rounds remain a separate computational parameter. These finite-ground results form the reference layer for the planned algorithm paper.

For universal presentations, computable bounds for either proof format are equivalent to decidability of the ground word problem. Sequential action-chain visibility has a further exact description by semigroup space and its contextual envelope. Keeping the sequential and congruence invariants distinct preserves that correspondence and specifies which guarantees the finite-ground algorithm actually computes.
