DESCRIPTION — COPY THE FOLLOWING THREE PARAGRAPHS
An incompletely specified action need not admit a completion on its original carrier, and its unspecified values need not remain independent. This paper studies finite two-sorted ground presentations consisting of complete native operation tables, carrier-compatibility equations, and arbitrary supplied action cells. For the pure-row fragment, the carrier congruence forced by initial semantics is characterized as the least quotient on which every row pushout preserves the distinguished carrier. Row protection is equivalent to homomorphic coherence and an exact kernel-extension condition. On the stabilized quotient Q, a row h:S→Q has forced domain S^κ, where κ is the congruence generated by the kernel of h.

The paper identifies the full equality relation on the named carrier and named action cells, including exact class counts at proof horizons one and two. Carrier-valued completion is characterized by a splitting of the carrier embedding into the row pushout. Finite examples show that quotient repairs need not have a least element, need not be upward closed, and can force more carrier equality than unrestricted models. Further results describe composition, group laws, and one-sorted coupling.

This is Paper I of a three-paper research programme on initial semantics, equality visibility, and checkable algorithmic certificates. The upload includes the English manuscript, LaTeX source, a standalone Python verification script, and its finite verification results. Companion Paper II is cited as an unpublished manuscript; Paper III is in preparation. The general theorems are supported by mathematical proofs; the included computations verify finite examples and comparisons.
