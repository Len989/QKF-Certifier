\section{Finite computation and reproducibility}\label{sec:computation}

The carrier and named-action diagnostics are finite even when the initial
algebra is infinite. For a finite set of observed terms $O$, take
\[
V=\operatorname{Sub}(E)\cup\operatorname{Sub}(O),
\]
where $\operatorname{Sub}(E)$ means the subterms of both sides of every
axiom. Congruence closure on the represented operation nodes computes
exactly the equality relation on $O$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:locality}.
There is no need to enumerate the entire term universe $T^{(2)}$.

The same finite construction can be filtered by depth. At horizon $D$,
retain nodes of depth at most $D$ and activate only equations whose two
sides are retained. Comparing the successive restrictions to $\mathcal I$
gives $\lambda_E$, $\chi_E$, and $\delta_E^{\mathcal I}$ where defined.
Recording the premises of each merge can produce equality derivations.
The finite partial quotient in the locality proof also gives a total
separating model for any unmerged query. These are standard uses of ground
congruence closure, not a new general equality decision procedure.

The structural feedback operator offers another route to the carrier
quotient. A raw row pushout can be queried on its finite carrier and row
tables without constructing its possibly infinite mixed-term part.
Iterate the resulting carrier kernels as in Proposition~\ref{prop:Phi}.
After stabilization, generate each $S_b$, compute $h_b$ and
$\Cg_{Q_E}(\ker h_b)$, and read off the forced domains and interface counts.
The agreement between this route and direct ground congruence closure is
an explicit consequence of Theorems~\ref{thm:leaststable}
and~\ref{thm:globalrows}.

Completion requires a separate finite search. For a candidate quotient
$Q=A/\theta$, enumerate maps $Q\to Q$, retain native endomorphisms, and
check the supplied values. Rows can be treated independently in the pure
fragment. With composition or group laws, impose the corresponding
constraints jointly on the chosen maps. This elementary search is useful
for small counterexamples; no polynomial bound for general repair search
is asserted here.

The supplementary source archive contains a self-contained Python script,
\path{verification/verify_article.py}, and its JSON results. It checks
the displayed operation tables, congruences, row kernels, interface
partitions, repair witnesses, and the sharp feedback family on finite
instances. It also compares two implementations of the named-interface
relation on a bounded collection of small presentations: input-subterm
congruence closure and row-copy propagation with carrier feedback.
These computations are reproducible evidence for the examples; the
universal statements rest on the proofs above.

This division of tasks sets the scope of the companion programme.
Paper~II~\cite{ShcherbakovII} studies presentation-sensitive visibility
beyond the small filtration used here. The planned Paper~III will relate
computational representations to their equality semantics and specify
independently checkable certificates. The associated software project is
\href{https://github.com/Len989/QKF-Certifier}{QKF-Certifier}.
The present paper does not identify that implementation with a complete
solver for every completion and repair problem considered here.
