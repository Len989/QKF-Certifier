\section{Kernel saturation and exact determinacy}\label{sec:kernelsat}

Assume now that $Q$ is a carrier quotient for which a row is protected, and suppress the subscript $b$.  Thus $S\le Q$, $h:S\to Q$ is a homomorphism,
\[
K=\ker h,
\qquad
\kappa=\Cg_Q(K),
\]
and $\kappa\cap S^2=K$.  Let
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
S & \hookrightarrow & Q_r\\
{\scriptstyle h}\downarrow && \downarrow{\scriptstyle p_r}\\
Q_* & \xrightarrow{m} & P
\end{array}
\]
be the stabilized row pushout.

\begin{theorem}[Kernel-saturation theorem]\label{thm:kernelsat}
In the protected row pushout above:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\ker p_r=\kappa$;
\item
\[
p_r(Q)\cap m(Q)=m(h(S));
\]
\item for $x\in Q$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forceddomain}
p_r(x)\in m(Q)
\iff
[x]_{\kappa}\cap S\neq\varnothing;
\end{equation}
\item if $x\equiv_{\kappa}s$ with $s\in S$, then
\[
p_r(x)=m(h(s)).
\]
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the exact carrier-forced domain of the row is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Db}
D=S^{\kappa}
:=
\{x\in Q:[x]_{\kappa}\cap S\neq\varnothing\}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Push out $S\hookrightarrow Q_r$ first along the quotient $S\twoheadrightarrow S/K$.  The result is $Q_r\twoheadrightarrow Q_r/\kappa$, and the induced copy of $S/K$ is embedded because $\kappa\cap S^2=K$.  The second stage is therefore the strong amalgam of the embeddings
\[
S/K\hookrightarrow Q_r/\kappa,
\qquad
S/K\hookrightarrow Q_*.
\]
Strong amalgamation gives both the row kernel and the exact intersection of the two images.  Statement \eqref{eq:forceddomain} is precisely the condition that the $\kappa$-class of $x$ belongs to the embedded copy of $S/K$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No hidden forcing for injective data]\label{cor:injective}
If $h:S\to Q$ is injective, then $\kappa=\Delta_Q$ and
\[
D=S.
\]
Thus an injective supplied partial homomorphism forces no additional row values outside its generated input subalgebra.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Exact number of external row classes]\label{cor:count}
In a finite protected row,
\[
|p_r(Q)\setminus m(Q)|
=
|Q/\kappa|-|S/K|.
\]
For an entirely unspecified row the contribution is $|Q|$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:kernelsat}, $p_r(Q)\cong Q/\kappa$ and its intersection with $m(Q)$ is isomorphic to $h(S)\cong S/K$.
\end{proof}

The formula shows that ``missing'' and ``free'' are not synonymous.  Nontrivial kernel propagation can make $D$ strictly larger than $S$, even equal to all of $Q$.
