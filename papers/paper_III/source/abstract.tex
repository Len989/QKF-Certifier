\begin{abstract}
QKF studies how incomplete observations determine computations through forced rows, their kernels, and compatible quotients. This paper develops that viewpoint into a certificate architecture for word programs. A consumer specifies the distinctions its proof needs; checked native facts label those distinctions; finite algebraic proofs establish the required action; and independently checked certificates connect that action to the source and the consumer.

We retain the exact finite relation-matrix kernel, the complete nine-row characterization of coordinatewise KnownBits, and the signed mask--interval case studies. A descending pass has a 49-state certificate for an exact masked maximum; an ascending pass factors three physical phases into two observations and supports a conditional lower-preservation contract. The revised architecture adds automatic observation selection within a finite source-derived language, a source-bound signed runtime, conditional residual coverage, and a separate source-to-consumer route that can prove a requested equality without first constructing the whole residual carrier. A pure-row checker computes initial forced domains, while a typed ground-DAG checker supplies chronological equality proofs and finite separating models. Checked closed lemmas, prepared contexts, and direct proof emission support reusable, executable summaries.

Lean 4 checks the earlier word fragments and, in two new packages, soundness of the decoded typed positive ground calculus and chronological composition of checked lemmas under explicit native premises. JSON decoding, source frontends, native source rules, and runtime correspondence remain outside those general formal results. Retained external results include 11 of 39 complete Nice to Meet You programs and 104 of 411 components; later development experiments do not enlarge those denominators. Controlled measurements show substantial reductions in preparation and proof-emission overhead, while QKF's own simpler ordinary-SDK route remains the faster registered baseline. These results separate the value of the certificate architecture from an unestablished performance advantage of its query backend.
\end{abstract}
