\section{Relation to previous work}

Nice to Meet You synthesizes practical MLIR abstract transformers and combines components by abstract meet~\cite{Peng2026,NTMYArtifact}. Its candidate validation uses Z3 over finite widths. QKF studies a parametric certificate route: source reconstruction and replay connect the program to a semantic interface, and a finite product or gluing argument lifts the interface check to every positive width. The original artifact, the total research contract, and LLVM's guarded or poison-producing operations require explicit semantic correspondence when their verdicts are compared.

KnownBits and the related tristate-number domain are established representations~\cite{LLVMKnownBits,Vishwanathan2022}. Their arithmetic properties motivate concrete native facts in this study. The contribution here concerns the specified finite semantic classes, source-bound proof architecture, and the use of consumer observations and forced rows to compose word-level obligations. The fixed-point and finite-state checks themselves use standard finite algorithms.

Bit-width-independent SMT approaches seek proofs through integer arithmetic, uninterpreted functions, and quantified encodings~\cite{Niemetz2019}. QKF currently succeeds where an adequate finite observation carrier and its semantic bridge can be constructed. Alive2 addresses LLVM refinement through bounded translation validation~\cite{Lopes2021}; its source model and obligations differ from the total transfer-expression contract here. The experiments do not establish that one of these approaches subsumes the others.

The algebraic organization is inherited from Papers I and II~\cite{ShcherbakovI,ShcherbakovII}. The comparable-pair principal-congruence formula used in Section~\ref{sec:sparse} is due to Martin-Maroto, Ricciardo, and de Polavieja~\cite{MartinMaroto2026}. Kernel factorization, inverse-image preservation, and induction are elementary ingredients. The applied step is to justify a particular native observation, retain its labels and domain, complete its rows under the applicable theory, and make those rows support the checked composition.

Lean supplies an external proof checker for the word fragments and the decoded typed proof/composition calculus~\cite{Lean4,LeanReference}. This reduces the trusted mathematical argument for those formalized fragments to its definitions, proof terms, and standard Lean assumptions. It does not automatically verify the surrounding parsers, Python implementations, or source-language bridges. The precise boundary is part of the result.
