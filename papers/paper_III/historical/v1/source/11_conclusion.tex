\section{Conclusion}
Initial semantics identifies the equalities forced by observations; equality visibility explains their finite proof interfaces. The present paper turns that line into explicit kernel contracts and a versioned account of the public tool. Full relation matrices compute the finite interface exactly, and sparse implementations have a concrete coverage and closure obligation. For the released KnownBits checker, sound local rewrites connect source text to a finite semantic quotient, where nine rows decide the supported target property at every positive width.

The distinction between those layers is operationally useful. It tells users what a successful certificate means and tells maintainers what must be proved when the algorithm changes. Future revisions can enlarge the supported core while retaining the release-specific evidence and citation history that make the software's scientific claims inspectable.
