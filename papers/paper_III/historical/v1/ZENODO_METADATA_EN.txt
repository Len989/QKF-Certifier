DESCRIPTION
This is Paper III of the QKF research series and the primary scientific reference for QKF-Certifier. It connects the initial semantics of incomplete actions in Paper I and the finite-ground equality-visibility results of Paper II to explicit algorithmic kernels and a versioned account of the public software.

For a finite ground specification of a separately compatible binary operation, the paper proves exactness and termination of full relation-matrix closure and gives a coverage theorem for sparse semilattice representations. These algebraic kernels are distinguished from the released word-program checker.

The public checker reconstructs expressions from a restricted straight-line transfer language, replays source-bound local rewrite certificates, and checks a coordinatewise KnownBits residual on the nine valid one-bit input rows. A product proof makes this check necessary and sufficient at every positive width. The paper gives the complete 18-bit semantic signature, the 64/64/16 sound precision classes for AND/OR/XOR, the additional monotone counts 26/26/16, all 29 rewrite rules, a decreasing termination measure, and the source-to-verdict composition theorem.

The described executable is QKF-Certifier v0.1.0a1 at commit f93561682319e8b911ed32c01583f2a496dc59fc, using qkf-rewrite-v3 certificates and the declared total-transfer-words-v2 semantic contract. The supplement contains LaTeX, research reference code, source-hash manifests, deterministic checks, generated certificates, and proposed repository citation files. The public repository is retrieved by immutable commit rather than duplicated in the supplement.

Fresh evidence includes 67 passing release tests, 804 relation/ground comparisons, 23,751 concrete rule-witness comparisons, exhaustive finite signature classifications, and 680 source mutations. Historical sparse C++ measurements are identified separately. The public release does not compute equality-visibility minima or implement a general arithmetic certifier. The manuscript is a preprint with mathematical proofs and finite implementation evidence; it is not a proof-assistant verification of the executable.
