QKF Certifier 0.2.0a1 — observations, word proofs and a Lean pilot

This research alpha brings the later QKF proof work into a reproducible source
release alongside the existing coordinatewise Python application. Paper III is
rewritten as manuscript 2.0, *Observation kernels and width-independent word proofs*.

- Consumer observations, checked contextual domains and labeled whole-word
  reconstruction are connected explicitly to the theory of forced rows, kernels
  and gluing from the first two papers.
- The frozen KnownBits profile replays all 39 original programs: **11 whole
  proofs and 104/411 component proofs**. Unsupported targets and contract-related
  refutations remain recorded.
- The Graal profile includes an all-width exact upper-bound model with emptiness
  distinction, and conditional lower-bound preservation of the joint mask/interval
  carrier. The lower theorem requires `lower <= 0` or a forbidden negative sign;
  it does not assert an exact minimum.
- A Lean 4.33.0 project verifies forced rows, a carry quotient, general gluing,
  the numerical masked-successor theorem and the modeled nonsign source-cell
  bridge, with an enforced axiom audit.
- A repository runner checks source/data hashes and replays saved research proofs
  in fresh processes. Research and Lean CI jobs accompany the existing package CI.

The installed `qkf` CLI keeps its AND/OR/XOR coordinatewise target scope, runtime
semantics and `qkf-rewrite-v3` certificate format. The wheel and ordinary sdist
contain that application; use the research source archive for the broader modules,
papers and Lean project.

Local candidate validation: 67 Python tests; complete 39-program research replay;
seven fresh Graal proof processes; clean 13-job Lean build; lint, package builds,
installed-wheel check and manuscript PDF review. Exact logs and scope are included.
Before publication, confirm successful GitHub Actions runs for the final commit.

This release does not establish full Graal `create` correctness, formal verification
of all Python/Java code, or general performance dominance over SMT. QKF-authored
code is MIT; upstream sources and manuscripts retain their separate notices.
